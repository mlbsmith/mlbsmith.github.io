title: On Doors
---

It feels like in this current cultural moment, as we try to figure out what to do with LLMs, agents, workflows, and loops, there are three doors ahead of us. Because this is a time of great change, and everyone is trying to figure out what to do with themselves.

Door one is trying to be a winner. In times of great technological change, the old order gets upset. Unexpected events happen constantly. And that means a lot of things are not priced correctly — to use a term from economics. The actual cost or value of something is not reflected in its price. The market is inefficient because no one knows what’s going on.

To be a winner, to go through door one, you are just trying to find a way to make the most money possible in this moment. It is the classic gold rush: trying to find the gold. It could also be the people selling the pickaxes. I don’t think those are mutually exclusive. The same type of people gravitate to a gold rush in different ways — everyone is trying to get the gold, but a lot of people are also selling the pickaxes.

Door two is trying not to be a loser. What I mean by that is: you’re worried about staying relevant. This is pretty modern, but the idea of getting laid off — one hundred years ago we didn’t even have labor laws, so simply having a job was crazy. Now we are pretty used to having jobs, and we are pretty used to having secure jobs, if you are a white-collar worker.

The fear of door number two. The philosophy is: don’t lose. Survive. Stay relevant. Play around with these tools. Just generally don’t be the worst at your company or in the bottom thirty percent.

Neither of these options feels optimal to me.

Door number one is a very low chance of success. The number of companies in the dot-com boom that survived was under ten percent, I think. The number of people who struck it rich in the gold rush was probably lower. The number of people trying to start factories during the Industrial Revolution — I assume that was pretty low too. (Maybe I should do some research on that one.)

Door number two is almost worse, though. Even though you have a higher chance of success, it feels fear-based and zero-sum. With door number one, you are not really competing, at least not in philosophy. The mindset is: there is a lot of money to be made here, or there is a lot to gain. I just want a piece of it.

Door number two is: somebody is going to lose. Somebody is going to get fired. Somebody is going to be replaced by a machine, have their loom taken away. It better not be me. You are actively competing with your coworkers, and that is not a good way to live either.

So we have door number one, which is I want to be a winner. Maybe the door of greed.

We have door number two, which is the door of fear.

What’s door number three?
