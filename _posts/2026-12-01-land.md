> It seems to me nonsense, in a period like our own, to think that one can avoid writing of such subjects.
> Everyone writes of them in one guise or another. It is simply a question of which side one takes and what approach one follows.

- George Orwell, Why I Write

The above quote is from Orwell as he describes why he, and everyone around him, is forced to write about totalitarianism.
I read this quote at perhaps the best time in my life to do so.
If we swap out "totalitarianism" for "artificial intelligence", you can apply his words, verbatim, to our current situation.

I had a brief moment of panic as I sat down to write this month because I worried I was writing about AI too much.
That worry was then vehemently counteracted by a gaping void of ideas when I eliminated anything tangentially related to AI from my possible essay topics.
It's a good thing I stumbled on Orwell's book on my shelf, otherwise I might never have been free of this paralysis.

My topic for today is not directly tied to AI as my previous posts have been.
Today, I'd like to creep up on the idea of technological independence.
This is something near and dear to my heart: 3acresandacow.com is an outgrowth of belief in this idea.

The world of ownership is fraught with historical baggage and misunderstandings.
There is a mythology tied to the idea of ownership that is a large part of the Western, and predominantly American, worldview.
This did not start with Americans of course -- they inherited this belief in personal property from the British.
The United States just has more land for people to own, so they have taken the idea of property rights to the ultimate limit.
Unfortunately, for our friends overseas, their tiny island isn't growing anytime soon.
This idea, while less "libertarian" here in Canada, is even more applicable because of the sheer land mass we have to work with.
Something like 90% of Canada is uninhabited.
We have enough space to share with the entire world.

Alas, not all land is inhabitable or desirable.
It is mostly the desirable part that is a blocker here.
No sane person wants to move into the middle of nowhere without electricity, running water, access to food, and most importantly, people.
So most of that 90% will sit empty until a time comes when we can convince large groups of people to move like the Canadian government did with the Mennonites fleeing the Soviet Union in 1920 [confirm?].

Good thing I'm not here to talk about physical land.
I'm here to talk about digital land.
The world is a strange place these days, and there are murmurs that we are in the end times.
Maybe we are, maybe we aren't, but I think we are in a very unique position.
I think most people, regardless of whether you think artificial intelligence is going to paperclip us into extinction, are anxious.
And, I think a lot of that anxiety comes from a feeling of impotence.
After all, what can I do if 2 or 3 American companies are spending trillions of dollars to build a super-intelligence that might render all human work and striving obsolete?

Today, I will try to answer that question for you.

Consider the simple website: it is a very basic thing that all of us use every day.
Yet it is a black box to most people, and a lot of companies (perhaps less now that AI is involved) will offer to set one up and maintain it for you for a small fee.
The thing is, it's not that complicated, and this touches on a very important concept that permeates the digital world: most of the internet is extremely basic.

Computers are extremely fast, and when connected together, they can do incredible things, but what they are doing is fundamentally just elementary level math: addition, subtraction, multiplication.
Now, I'm not going to start from ground zero here and build all the way up to the physics of the internet, but what I will do is explain why the world doesn't need to be a black box to you, and why you should own your digital life.

We have all been convinced that big tech companies offer us services that cannot be achieve alone.
And the price we pay for that is ownership.
For most of the important things we store online - photos, documents, messages - we are renting the use of those applications for the monthly, nay daily, cost of our data.
This is the same data that advertisers use to track you all over the internet and monitor your behaviour with the intent of building the perfect persona that they can then sell to other companies who send targeted ads your way.
And it's the same data that all the frontier AI labs used to train their models, which they have leveraged to the tune of approximately a gazillion dollar valuations.

But I'm here to tell you that that you can achieve these things alone.
You do not need to trade your personal information for convenience.
There is another way.
A way that requires you to gain (gasp) competence.
Competence is very cool, trust me on that.
So, if you like owning your digital life and you like being competent, please continue with me on this journey.
